Why we started

We wanted the check we couldn’t find.

It started with one of us setting up an agent, just to understand the shift and get the promised productivity. By the time it worked, the cost was clear: we’d be giving an agent blind access to our world.

So we asked around. CTOs, engineers, friends building with agents. Same answer every time. The gains are real, and so is the pressure. They sign off, but they sign off nervous, because there is no way to check that the agent will do what it’s told.

And the risk isn’t only attackers. An agent no one has touched can still drift from its instructions, misread the context, and act with confidence on a wrong conclusion.

So we built the check we wanted: will the agent do what it’s told, and only that?

The science

The idea isn’t new. Pointing it at agents is.

Spotting the moment behavior stops looking like itself is how fraud teams have long caught account takeover. One of us spent years on that work. We pointed it at agents, and kept AI models out of the decision.

Questions

What investors ask.

Why not use AI to guard AI?

A guard that reads the same text as the agent can be misled the same way. And it adds more models, more tokens and more GPUs. We score behavior with math instead.

What does it cost to run?

No GPU and no tokens. It runs on a lightweight CPU, on the customer’s machine.

Who is it for?

Small and mid-sized teams running agents, especially those without a security department.

Why now?

The weight

No AI. No GPU. No tokens.
